This presentation explores the importance of endogeneity in causal inference and introduces the Difference-in-Differences (DiD) method. We discuss the empirical application of DiD in a shock-based setting, emphasizing key validity conditions required for reliable estimation. Real-world examples illustrate how DiD helps identify causal effects by comparing affected and unaffected groups before and after policy implementation. The discussion highlights the necessity of ensuring parallel trends and addressing potential biases to draw valid conclusions from observational data.
