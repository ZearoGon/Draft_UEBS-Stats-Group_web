Zhengnan replicates the main results of Ehsani and Linnainmaa (2022, JF), confirming that time-series factor momentum is widespread across the US and Global factor cross-sections, and extends their sample by five years through December 2024 to show the result is stable post-publication, including across the COVID period. He then asks whether the paper's mechanism - ten principal components of forty-seven factors - requires that full machinery. Focusing on Betting-Against-Beta and Quality-Minus-Junk, he finds that a 2-factor PCA on these two well-known factors alone is sufficient to explain the momentum cross-section: the winners-minus-losers spread becomes statistically insignificant once their PC1-TSFM is included, in sharp contrast to FF5 plus UMD, which leaves a significant residual. He offers a structural reading of this result: momentum winners drift toward low-β and high-quality, losers toward the opposite, so PC1-TSFM of BAB and QMJ is the natural minimal carrier of the UMD-pricing kernel. The finding is consistent with Kozak, Nagel, and Santosh's (2020) low-dimensionality view of the cross-section, and shows that the apparent ubiquity of factor momentum can coexist with sharp concentration in its pricing power.
